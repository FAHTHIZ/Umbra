Privacy trade-off

Page inspection is local and does not send the current address or page contents to a service. Enabling ad or tracker blocking can make your browser behave differently from other Tor Browser users, which may make you more distinguishable. Keep it off for higher-anonymity browsing.

Detection is heuristic. A detected provider is not a payment endorsement; a warning is not proof of fraud. Umbra never reads form values, card numbers, passwords or authentication tokens. Find public alternatives

Search only for text you enter. Umbra does not attach the current page address, onion hostname or page contents.

Search results are untrusted. Verify sources independently. OnionSearch is a third-party provider: when you submit, it receives the query and connection metadata through Tor. Its privacy policy describes automatic collection of browser and operating-system information, IP address and timestamps. Tor normally makes that IP the Tor exit address rather than your source IP; it does not make the query itself secret from the provider. Do not search for a whistleblower's identity or enter sensitive investigation details. Read OnionSearch's privacy policy.
